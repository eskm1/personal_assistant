# Studio playbook

The rules the idea generator and the editor follow on every post. Edit freely: change a
rule when it stops fitting, delete what you disagree with. Notes you mark "Remember for
future edits" on the dashboard are added at the bottom.

Starting rules adapted from the editing playbook in Nate Herk's Edit Kit
(github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit, MIT), trimmed to what fits renovation and
personal footage. They are a starting point, not law.

## Story

- The first 3 seconds decide everything: open on a claim, a result or a surprising
  visual that makes someone want the ending. Name the topic in that opening.
- One idea per post. Hook, a line of context, the proof or process, then the payoff.
- Close the loop you opened: if the hook asks "how", the post shows how.
- Never invent results, prices, timelines or claims the footage and Bryan's words don't support.
- End on a complete sentence, then a short beat on the last shot and the Urban Makers end card; never stop mid-sentence or mid-animation.

## Pacing and cutting

- Cut between phrases, never inside one. Keep natural beats after a comma or "first,".
- Remove dead air between phrases, but don't speed up the voice.
- When a line was said twice, keep the last clean take.
- Shots of B-roll average 1 to 2 seconds; nothing static lingers past 3 seconds.
- Every shot should move: real footage, a slow push-in, or an action happening.

## Formats

- Talking head: cut between sentences and ideas, keep the natural pauses inside a sentence; trim only long silences. Captions on every phrase, B-roll over the words it illustrates.
- Day in the life: music-led, short clips in time order, a few spoken lines as anchors.
- Compilation: cut to the music's beat; before/after reveals land on a beat.
- Urban Makers: show the work and the result. Close-ups of craft, the finished space,
  the team doing the job. Credibility over hype.
- Personal: Bryan's voice and perspective first; footage supports what he's saying.

## Captions

- 1 to 3 words at a time, split on phrase boundaries, timed to the spoken word.
- One caption style per post. Bold sans-serif, high contrast, on a subtle dark box so it
  reads on any background.
- Never cover the mouth, the work being shown, or text in the shot.

## Music and sound

- Music sits well under the voice and dips while Bryan speaks.
- Pick by mood: confident beat for results and reveals, calm groove for process and how-to.
- Sound effects only for visible actions, and sparingly.
- Final loudness around -14 LUFS; never fade the last spoken word.

## Checks before a draft goes to review

- Every word at each cut is whole (re-transcribe the render and compare).
- Captions match the audio and never sit over a face.
- The first frame is meaningful on its own.
